Civil Services (UPSC)

The prestigious journey to becoming an IAS, IPS, or IFS officer.

IAS / IPS (UPSC CSE)

Serve the nation through the elite Indian Administrative Service or Indian Police Service. This is one of the toughest examinations in the world.

Step 1

Graduation & Eligibility

The basic requirement for the civil services.

  • Complete a Bachelor's degree in any discipline
  • Ensure you meet the age limit criteria (usually 21-32 years, with category relaxations)
Step 2

Phase 1: Prelims Examination

The initial screening test (Objective Type).

  • General Studies Paper 1 (History, Geography, Polity, Economy, Current Affairs)
  • CSAT Paper 2 (Aptitude, Reasoning, Comprehension) - Qualifying nature
  • Develop a strong daily newspaper reading habit (The Hindu / Indian Express)
Step 3

Phase 2: Mains Examination

The subjective, in-depth written examination.

  • Essay Paper (Highly scoring if practiced well)
  • 4 General Studies Papers (GS I, II, III, IV - Ethics)
  • 2 Optional Subject Papers (Choose based on your strength and interest)
  • Extensive answer writing practice is the secret to clearing Mains
Step 4

Phase 3: Personality Test (Interview)

The final assessment of your administrative traits.

  • Interview by the UPSC board in New Delhi (Dholpur House)
  • Assessment of mental alertness, critical powers of assimilation, clear and logical exposition, and leadership
Step 5

Academy Training

Preparation for active duty.

  • Foundation course at LBSNAA, Mussoorie for all services
  • IAS officers continue at LBSNAA for Phase I and Phase II training
  • IPS officers proceed to SVPNPA, Hyderabad for rigorous police training

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the success rate of the UPSC exam?
The success rate is notoriously low, typically around 0.1% to 0.2%. Approximately 1,000,000 candidates apply every year, about 500,000 actually appear for the Prelims, and only about 800 to 1,000 are finally selected for various services.
Do I need to go to Delhi for coaching?
While Delhi (specifically areas like Rajinder Nagar and Mukherjee Nagar) is the hub for UPSC coaching, it is no longer mandatory. With the rise of high-quality online platforms, massive telegram communities, and digital resources, many candidates are now clearing the exam from their hometowns through self-study.
Which optional subject is the best?
There is no "best" optional subject. You should choose a subject based on: 1) Your interest in it, 2) Your background (graduation subject), and 3) Availability of study material and guidance. Popular choices include Sociology, PSIR, Anthropology, and Geography.
