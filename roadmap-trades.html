Vocational & Skilled Trades

Highly respected, hands-on careers that keep the world running.

Electrician

Install, maintain, and repair electrical power, communications, lighting, and control systems.

Step 1

Basic Education & Safety

  • High School Diploma or equivalent (focus on algebra and physics)
  • Learn fundamental electrical theory (Ohm's Law, circuits)
  • Crucial: Master OSHA safety standards and first-aid
Step 2

Apprenticeship Program

The core of trade education. Earn while you learn.

  • Typically 4-5 years of paid, on-the-job training
  • Requires hundreds of hours of classroom instruction alongside field work
  • Learn to read blueprints and understand the National Electrical Code (NEC)
Step 3

Journeyman License

  • Pass the Journeyman Electrician Exam in your state/region
  • You are now certified to work independently without direct supervision
Step 4

Master Electrician (Optional)

  • After several years as a Journeyman, take the Master exam
  • Allows you to pull permits, design systems, and run your own contracting business

Carpenter / Woodworker

Construct, repair, and install building frameworks and structures made from wood and other materials.

Step 1

Foundational Skills

  • Math skills (geometry and fractions are critical)
  • Familiarity with basic hand and power tools
  • High school diploma or vocational tech school certification
Step 2

Apprenticeship or Helper Role

  • Join a union apprenticeship (typically 3-4 years) or work as a carpenter's helper
  • Learn framing, roofing, interior finish, and blueprint reading
Step 3

Specialization

  • Rough Carpentry (Framing, structural work)
  • Finish Carpentry (Cabinetry, molding, detailed aesthetics)
  • Become a licensed Journeyman Carpenter

Frequently Asked Questions

Are skilled trades a good career choice?
Absolutely. While many push for college degrees, there is a massive global shortage of skilled tradespeople. This high demand means excellent job security, strong wages, and the ability to start a career without massive student debt.
How physically demanding are these jobs?
Trades can be very physically demanding. You will spend a lot of time on your feet, lifting heavy materials, and working in varied weather conditions. Proper ergonomic practices and physical fitness are important for a long career.
