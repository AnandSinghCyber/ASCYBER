Law & Justice

The path to becoming an Advocate, arguing cases, and upholding the law.

Lawyer / Advocate (India)

Represent clients in legal proceedings, draft legal documents, and advise on legal rights and responsibilities.

Step 1

Educational Qualification (LLB)

The foundational law degree.

  • Option A: 5-Year Integrated LLB (BA LLB / BBA LLB) right after 10+2. Requires clearing entrance exams like CLAT, AILET, or LSAT.
  • Option B: 3-Year LLB after completing a Bachelor's degree in any other discipline.
  • Focus on subjects like Constitutional Law, Criminal Procedure Code (CrPC), Indian Penal Code (IPC), and Contracts.
Step 2

Internships & Moot Courts

Building practical skills during law school.

  • Participate heavily in Moot Court competitions to build argumentation and research skills.
  • Complete mandatory internships under practicing advocates, law firms, or NGOs to understand court procedures and drafting.
Step 3

State Bar Council Enrollment

Becoming an Advocate.

  • After receiving your LLB degree, you must enroll with a State Bar Council to get a provisional license to practice law.
Step 4

All India Bar Examination (AIBE)

The final licensing hurdle.

  • Within two years of state enrollment, you must clear the AIBE conducted by the Bar Council of India (BCI).
  • Clearing this grants you the 'Certificate of Practice', making you a full-fledged Advocate eligible to practice in courts across India.
Step 5

Career Paths

Choosing your specialization.

  • Litigation (Practicing in District Courts, High Courts, or the Supreme Court)
  • Corporate Law (Working in Law Firms, Mergers & Acquisitions, Contracts)
  • Judiciary (Clearing state judicial services exams to become a judge)

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between a Lawyer and an Advocate?
In India, anyone with a law degree (LLB) is a 'Lawyer'. However, only a lawyer who has enrolled with a Bar Council and cleared the AIBE is termed an 'Advocate' and has the right to represent clients in a court of law.
Is corporate law more lucrative than litigation?
Initially, yes. Corporate lawyers joining top-tier law firms usually start with very high salaries right out of college. Litigation takes years of struggle and building a client base, but successful senior advocates in litigation can earn significantly more in the long run than corporate partners.
